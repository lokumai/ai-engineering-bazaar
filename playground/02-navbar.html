Navbar — three alternatives

All three are shown in one neutral palette on purpose, so you are judging the structure and not the colour. Every one of them is identical on every page, has a visible current location, and puts search and language in the same place. Pick a letter.

The dropdown is shown open so you can see it. Hovering or clicking “Curriculum” reveals the five levels with your count beside each.

Why it works: four words, one open menu, and the level list is one hover away rather than a separate page. Cost: a dropdown is a hover target, so it needs a tap-friendly version on mobile.

The second row changes as you move; the first row never does. Progress sits at the right of the second row, so it is always visible without taking a whole panel.

Why it works: it answers “where am I” and “how far along am I” on every single page, permanently. Cost: 96px of vertical space before any content, and two rows read as heavier.
C

Levels as the navigation

the course pattern: Duolingo, roadmap.sh, Exercism
LBazaar
Fundamentals 3/8 Intermediate 0/8 Expert 0/11 Ecosystem 0/5 Protocols 0/1

There is no “Curriculum” link, because the five levels are the curriculum. Everything else becomes an icon on the right.

Why it works: the structure of the course is the navigation, so a beginner sees the whole shape immediately, and the colour code is learned in the first second. Cost: five level names will not fit on a phone, so mobile needs a different treatment, and the icons on the right carry no labels.

My read: A if the priority is that nobody has to learn anything, B if the priority is that a reader always knows where they are and how far along, C if the priority is that the course structure teaches itself. A and B combine cleanly: A's dropdown can sit in B's first row.
