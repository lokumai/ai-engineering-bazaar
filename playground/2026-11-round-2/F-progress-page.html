What should the progress page keep, now that progress is kept per track?

Three slim versions of /profile/. Each one keeps the five level cards exactly as they look today, and shows realistic progress: For Engineers has 7 of 33 done (5 of 8 Fundamentals), For Everyone has 2 of 33. Every mark below can be clicked.

  • F1 Tabs, nothing else
  • F2 Tabs and a data line · Recommended
  • F3 Both tracks stacked

Gone in all three: the account block (name, Checked by, the approval marks, seeds), Across browsers, Waiting on you, Readout, Streak, Stamps, What you built, Role and path, The curriculum as one diagram, Record of work, and the five settings rows. The three numbers row is gone too: Days in a row is a streak, and Reading time is a sum nobody acts on. Modules completed survives as the count beside each track's name, where it tells you which track is further along.

F1 Tabs, nothing else

Two tabs under the heading, one per track, each with its count. Under them, the five level cards. That is the whole page.

  • The shortest the page can be: one screen on a laptop, and nothing on it that nobody uses.
  • The page opens on the track you are reading in (the one the top bar shows). The tabs only change the view; they do not switch your track.
  • No way to save or move your progress. If the browser clears its storage, both tracks are gone, and the page never told you that could happen.

F2 Tabs and a data line Recommended

F1, plus one quiet line at the bottom: your progress lives in this browser, with three actions to back it up, restore it, or reset it.

Recommended: progress lives only in the browser, so one line that lets a reader keep a copy is worth its space.

  • The progress is never sent anywhere, which is good for privacy, but a browser can delete it without asking: clearing site data, a private window, or Safari after seven days without a visit. A backup file is the only thing that survives that.
  • Restore also moves progress to another browser or computer, which is the one useful thing the removed sign-in did.
  • Costs one line of small text below the legend. Reset clears both tracks and asks once before it does; it cannot be undone without a backup.

F3 Both tracks stacked

No tabs. The page shows For Engineers with its five cards, then For Everyone with its five cards, each under its own heading and count.

  • Everything is visible at once, with no control to learn. Good for a reader who really does both tracks.
  • The page is twice as long, and most readers stay in one track, so half of it is usually a wall of empty rings.
  • The two lists name the same 33 modules, so the eye has to check which heading it is under before ticking. F2's data line can sit under this one as well.
