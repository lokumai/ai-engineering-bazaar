How should the street ask a reader to choose a track before the five doors?

A new step in the home page's walk, after the four stalls and before the gate. The reader picks For Engineers or For Everyone, and the doors then open into that track. Nothing is saved: the next walk down the street asks again.

  • Holding: the walk stops at the choice for about one screen of scrolling. A click plays the rest of the walk and opens the doors.
  • Never a trap: a reader who keeps scrolling still reaches the gate, and the doors work (see N1 and N2).
  • Each picture below is a 1920 by 1080 screen, scaled. Click a choice in it to see the doors open.

H1

Two shop signs over two stalls

Recommended

Two stalls side by side, each under a hanging sign that names the track and says who it is for. The sign is the button.

It reuses the street's sign-and-stall pattern, keeps a readable sentence for each track, and stacks cleanly on a phone.

  • Uses the walk's own grammar: the four stalls before it already pair a sign with a stall, so it needs no new kind of picture.
  • Holds the most text, so each track can say in a full sentence who it is for.
  • On a phone it stacks into two signs, the same way the stalls already do.
  • Close to the four stalls before it, so it relies on the question plaque and the button to read as a choice and not a fifth motto.

H2

A fork in the street with two lit archways

The street splits around a column into two archways, each lit from inside, with a sign over each. Clicking one walks the reader through it to the gate.

  • The strongest picture of "choose a way": two openings and a light at the end of each.
  • The zoom through the arch makes the click feel like stepping in.
  • Two separate streets suggest two separate places, but both lead to the same gate of five doors.
  • The archways are large targets, but what they mean is written only on the signs above them.

H3

A host at the counter with two trays

A merchant behind one wide counter offers two trays of lokum, each with a tag that names the track. The chosen tray lifts, and the walk goes on to the doors.

  • Warm and personal: a person offers the choice, in the bazaar's own gesture.
