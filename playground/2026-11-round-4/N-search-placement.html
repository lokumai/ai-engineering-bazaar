Where should search go, and what should it show?

Milestone M56, issue #43. Search has to find a module or a level by its name and by what its pages say, in English, Turkish and Persian. Each option below is the real bar, working on the real module titles and the real text of the 19 written modules. Type in it, open its advanced panel, and change the width to see where the bar gives way (1440, 1180, 1024, 880, 767 and 390 are the widths where the live bar changes).

Four decisions for you

  1. The placement. Pick one of N1 to N6 (or combine, for example N1 on a wide screen and N4's icon on a phone). Recommended: N1, your own idea, with / and Ctrl K also opening it.
  2. The engine. Page O measures the options on the real corpus. It is independent of the placement: any option here can sit on any engine.
  3. The default scope. When a reader opens search, does it look only at their own track and language, or at everything? Recommended: their own track and language, with the other tracks and languages one click away. Try both with the setting below.
  4. Unwritten For Everyone modules. Today none of the For Everyone modules is written. Should search leave them out? Recommended: leave them out, so no result opens a page that says "not written yet". Try both with the setting below.

Write back the codes, for example "N1, scope: own track and language, unwritten left out".

N1

A wide centred field in the bar, with an advanced dropdown

Recommended

Your own idea. A white field sits in the middle of the bar, between the menu and the track switch. Click it, or press / or Ctrl K, and a panel drops down: the advanced filters first (track, level, language, and whether to search titles, prose or code), then the results as you type.

  • Search is on every page, in the place readers look for it, and needs no new page.
  • Room is the cost. Measured on this bar: 338px free at 1440, 276px at 1180, 120px at 1024 and 218px at 880. With the site name hidden beside the logo the field is 548px at 1440. At 390px the bar has 3px free, so the field becomes a second row and the bar is 104px tall instead of 58px.
  • A dropdown has no address, so a search cannot be linked to. Results open on Enter or a click.
  • The field's fill is your choice. White is 13.3:1 against the bar. The mockup's own translucent field with white text has a border at 2.2:1 against the bar (the same border the bar's other buttons have), and its text is 10.5:1 on its fill.

Why recommended: it is the pattern readers already know from documentation sites, it is always visible, and the two keys make it as quick as a palette (N2). The narrow-screen second row is the one real cost, and N4's icon can replace it on a phone if you prefer.

Keyboard

/ or Ctrl K focuses the field from anywhere, and focus opens the panel. ↓ ↑ move through the results while the field keeps focus (a combobox with aria-activedescendant), Enter opens the active one, Tab walks the filters (each a toggle button), Esc closes the panel and a second Esc clears the field. The count is announced in a live region.

Seen on

GitHub's search field in the top bar, MDN's header field, and Algolia DocSearch (a field in the bar that opens a panel).

How one result reads

  • 1The module's title, with the matched words marked and underlined, so colour is not the only signal. Beside it a "Draft" tag when the module is not written.
  • 2A snippet of about 170 characters around the first match, with an ellipsis where it is cut.
  • 3The level (its name beside its colour key), the track with its icon and name, the language, the minutes.
  • 4Where it matched (title, section heading, text or code), the section, and how many more matches the module has.

N2

A palette on a key, opened from an icon in the bar

The bar gets one 33px magnifier, like its other icons. Ctrl K (⌘ K on a Mac) or / opens the same thing: a centred panel over a dimmed page with the field on top, the filters under it and the results below.

  • Costs the bar almost nothing: 33px at every width, and the field and results get a wide panel to themselves. At 390px the navigation narrows to 124px and scrolls 36px sideways, which loses today's guarantee that every destination is in view.
  • The panel has room for a longer result with a snippet and for the filters, so it suits a corpus with lots of text.
  • Hidden until learned: a reader has to notice the icon, and the keys help only readers who know palettes. No address, so a search cannot be linked to.

Keyboard

Ctrl K or / opens it, and Ctrl K closes it again. It is a dialog with a focus trap: Tab cycles field, filters, field. ↓ ↑ and Enter work as in N1. Esc closes it and focus returns to the icon. The page behind is not reachable while it is open.

Seen on

The command palettes of Linear, Notion and GitHub, and the search dialog of many documentation sites.

N3

A search page of its own

The bar gets a magnifier link to /search/. The page has a large field, the advanced filters in a column beside the results, and every result as a card. The address carries the search, so it can be bookmarked and shared (the grey line under the title shows it).

  • The most room for a result: a card can hold a long snippet, and the filters are always visible at desktop width.
  • A search can be linked to, and the browser's Back button returns to it.
  • It is a new route and a page per language, and a reader leaves the page they were on. At 390px the filters sit behind a "Filters" button. The bar has the same 33px cost as N2 (narrowing the navigation to 124px at 390).

Keyboard

The page is an ordinary form, so it needs nothing special: the field, the Search button, the filter toggles, then the results as links in reading order. / focuses the field from anywhere on the page. The address updates with the search.

Wikipedia's search results page, MDN's search page, and most shop and library sites.

N4

An icon in the bar that expands into a field

A 33px magnifier sits in the bar. Pressing it expands a field in the place of the navigation (Home, Catalog and Progress step aside while it is open), with a Close button at its end. The results and the advanced filters drop down under it, aligned to the right edge.

  • At rest the bar costs 33px, and while open the field has the whole middle of the bar: 536px at 1440 and 260px at 390.
  • The navigation disappears while searching, which is fine for a moment and surprising for a reader who wanted to switch page.
  • Two steps (press the icon, then type) where N1 needs one. At 390px, at rest, it has the same 124px navigation as N2.

Keyboard

Enter or Space on the icon expands it and moves focus to the field; / and Ctrl K do the same. Arrows and Enter work as in N1. Esc or Close collapses it and focus returns to the icon, so a keyboard reader is back where they were.

Seen on

The top app bar in Google's Material Design (a search icon that expands into a field).

N5

A search field in the catalog's filter bar

Nothing changes in the bar. The Catalog page gets a field above its list of modules, and the advanced filters open from a Filters button beside it. With nothing typed the page shows every module as today; with a query it shows the results as cards.

  • The bar keeps all its room at every width, and the track tabs already on the page set the track.
  • Search is only on the catalog. A reader on a module page has to go to the catalog first, so it does not answer "find the page that mentions X" from where they are.
  • Can be combined with another option: N5 as the home of the filters, and N1 or N4 as the way in from every page.

Keyboard

An ordinary form: Tab to the field, then the Filters button, the filter toggles, then each result as a link. / focuses the field on the catalog. The count is announced in a live region.

Seen on

Course catalogs and model hubs, where a field and a filter column sit above or beside a grid of cards.

N6

A search field at the top of the curriculum sidebar

The left sidebar that lists the levels on module and catalog pages gets a field at its top. Focusing it opens a panel over the page, with the filters and the results, the same panel as N1 but hung from the sidebar.

  • It sits where a reader already looks for another module, and the bar is not touched.
  • Today the sidebar does not exist under 880px (the design language hides it), and it is not on Home or Progress. So on a phone, search would need a new drawer opened from a button (two taps), which this demo draws as "Curriculum and search".
  • Search sits behind the sidebar's fold: a reader who folded the sidebar has also folded search.

Keyboard

/ focuses the field (and opens the drawer on a narrow screen). Focus opens the panel; arrows, Enter and Esc work as in N1, and Esc returns focus to the field. Tab from the field walks the filters.

Seen on

The sidebar search box of Read the Docs themes, and MDN's sidebar filter field.
