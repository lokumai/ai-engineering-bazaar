Round 4

A Turkish woman for the gate

The cast has a foreign man, a foreign woman and a Turkish man, and no Turkish woman. Leyla, the spice seller, gives up her place. Pick one of five, A to E, and she takes it; nothing on the site has changed yet. Each is drawn by the same hand as the seven (the same 220 by 400 grid, stroke weight, palette, face and robe), in the dress of old Istanbul and with a trade prop in her hand (D93).

  • A, B and C are the bazaar's own traders, each with a trade and a prop: a bohçacı with her knotted bundle, an embroiderer at her hoop, a lokum maker with a copper tray.
  • D and E are inspired by two women of Turkish letters, in the dress of their own time: Mihrî Hatun of Amasya (15th century) and Fatma Aliye (1862 to 1936). They are drawn as period figures with a pen and pages, not as portraits.
  • Each story is two sentences, about the length of the seven, with a gentle idea from the course in it. The story is English, as on the home page (D80); a Turkish and a Persian draft sit under each in case that changes.
  • The candidates keep to the street's rules: a front view, a back view and a run (a prop that must stay level does not swing), and each fits under the same caption chip as the others.
