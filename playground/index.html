The four themes

The same module page rendered four ways, so you are comparing like with like. Each is a different UI language, not a different colour scheme.

T1 Handbook

The documentation lens: MDN, Stripe, Astro. Permanent left nav, one accent colour, nothing to learn.

Safest, most familiar T2 Reader

The Medium lens. No side rails at all, serif body, contents in a drawer, a progress line under the bar.

Best for pure reading T3 Workbench

The dev-tool lens: Linear, Vercel, Raycast. Dark by default, compact, command palette.

Snappiest, most technical T4 Bazaar

Built from your banner. Every colour sampled from assets/banner-tiles.jpeg: cobalt, cream, clay, ochre, teal. A tile band is the one ornament.

The only one that looks like you
On T4. Cobalt leads rather than the cream, and the headings are a geometric sans rather than a serif, on purpose: cream plus serif plus a clay accent is the single most common machine-generated look right now, and it would have made your own artwork read as a template. The ornament is spent once, on the tile band, and every other surface is quiet.

Bazaar, four grounds — open for a decision

T4's palette is unchanged: cobalt #282864, clay #A0503C, ochre #C8A078, teal #2F8C86, gold #B8873B. One token moves, the ground — the fill behind the page, which is also the fill of the active item in the navbar. Today it is #F4ECE0 at 84.6% relative luminance; these four run 89.3% to 96.6%.

What moves with the ground, and why. Only the raised surface. A card cannot be darker than the page it sits on, so --paper goes to white on G2, G3 and G4. Nothing else in the palette changes, and the active navbar chip gains contrast against the cobalt bar for free: 11.36:1 today, up to 12.88:1 on G3.
What changed besides the ground. All four also carry the three fixes you asked for, so you are judging the real thing: the content column is centred and spans its container with gutters that clear both rails, the module list folds away in 200ms with a tab on the left edge to bring it back, the completion tick is a filled disc rather than a hairline glyph, and the level you are in is enlarged with a coloured edge.
Measured while building these. The teal tick sits at 3.44:1 against today's cream and only reaches 3.90:1 on the lightest ground, so lightening the page cannot fix it on its own. As a filled disc with a white check it is a graphical indicator rather than text, which needs 3:1, and it clears that on every ground. Keeping teal as a hairline glyph would not.
